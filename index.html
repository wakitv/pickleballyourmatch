How PickleMatch Works

1. Fair Rotation

Whoever has played the fewest games always plays next, and among equals the longest-waiting player goes first. Nobody is skipped to improve a pairing.

2. Partner Rotation

Recent teammate combinations are avoided first. A previous teammate can become your partner again after enough rotation.

3. Opponent Rotation

Recent opponents are also deprioritized, preventing the same matchups from repeating too quickly.

4. Skill-Based Doubles Pairing

Stronger players are paired with weaker ones (3.0 + 2.0, 3.0 + 2.5), never all the strong players on one side. Among equally good splits the pairing is picked at random, and combined team ratings stay close.

5. Manual Court Control

Choose 1 or 2 courts in Match Settings. Generate Matches only fills the selected number of available courts. No match is generated automatically.

6. Fair Leaderboard

Players start at different ratings, so rank is based on results, not the starting number. Score is your win rate adjusted for games played, so a 1-0 start cannot beat someone who has won 7 of 9. Ties go to point difference, then wins.

7. PDDR Doubles Rating

PickleMatch uses its own 2.00–8.00 Dynamic Doubles Rating. It considers team strength, expected result, score performance, match margin and experience. First 5 games are provisional. It is not an official DUPR rating.

8. Games per Player

In Match Settings you can cap how many games each player gets, e.g. 5 games each. Once a player reaches the limit they are marked Done and left out of new matches. When fewer than 4 players still have games left, the session is finished. If players × games is not divisible by 4, a few players may end one game short.

9. Court Skill Levels

Each court can be reserved for a rating band — for example Court 1 for beginners (up to 2.9) and Court 2 for 3.0 and up. Generate Matches then only picks players whose rating fits that court, and a CHANGE substitute must fit it too. Fair rotation still applies inside each band: among the players a court accepts, the one with the fewest games goes on next. A match already in play is never changed when you edit a level. Turn it off to go back to any player on any court.

10. Fixed Partners

In Match Settings you can lock two players together as partners for the rest of the session — they are always on the same team. A locked pair is picked as one unit: both play or neither does, so if one partner is Done on the game limit or does not match a court's level, the pair waits. The "avoid recent partners" rule is switched off for them, while fair rotation still decides when they play. CHANGE can split a pair for one round if you need to, and UNLINK in Match Settings ends the partnership for good.
