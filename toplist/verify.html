What this check proves

And what it deliberately does not
The badge is checked twice

First against the checksum baked into its own QR, which catches a badge whose name, rank or record has been edited. Then against the organiser's spreadsheet, which is what makes it official rather than merely intact.

The spreadsheet is the record

Every badge here was written when the session ended. Adding to that sheet needs both the sheet itself and the organiser's secret phrase, which never leaves their own browser.

The fingerprint is recomputed

This page does not take the spreadsheet's word for a row. It rebuilds the badge payload and hashes it again, so a row edited by hand after the fact fails here even though the sheet still holds it.

What it is not

BuenasxPickle runs entirely in a browser with no server, so a badge cannot be cryptographically signed: any key sent to a browser is public. A badge that passes its checksum but is missing from the sheet is unverified, not certified.
